How we start and run a contract

This is what happens from the first walkthrough through each month of service.

Starting a contract

  1. We walk the property with you

    Jose or your account lead walks the grounds with you or your manager. We look at turf, plants, trees and irrigation, and ask what's been frustrating you.

  2. You get a written proposal

    Scope, visit schedule, a list of what the contract covers, and a monthly price. Recommended improvements are listed separately, so you can decide on them later.

  3. We map the property

    Before the first visit, we record every irrigation zone, controller and valve, note the plants and trees, and set the seasonal schedule for your site. The plan on our home page shows what this map looks like.

  4. The first month brings the property up to standard

    Early visits focus on catching up: fixing irrigation problems, cleaning up beds and bringing the property to the standard we maintain from then on.

  5. Monthly walkthroughs and reports

    Every month we walk the property with you and send a written report. Together the reports form a record of all work done on the property.

The Phoenix landscape calendar

The work we schedule each month on a typical HOA or commercial property, and why it happens when it does.

January

Frost protection on tender plants. Dormant pruning of deciduous trees. Controllers on winter schedules.

Cold nights in the low 30s can damage citrus, lantana and bougainvillea.

February

for spring weeds. Rose and perennial cutbacks. Irrigation check as growth resumes.

Applying pre-emergent before soil warms stops weeds before they sprout.

March

Spring planting. Shrub pruning after the last frost. Controllers stepped up.

Mild weather gives new plants time to root before summer.

April

Full irrigation check, zone by zone. Fertilize as bermuda greens up.

Fixing problems now costs less than replacing plants lost in June.

May

from winter rye to . Pre- tree thinning begins.

Rye fades in the heat; bermuda needs room and water to take over.

June

Early crew starts for heat safety. Palm trimming after flowering. Longer run times.

The hottest, driest month of the year. Drip systems are checked often.

July

Monsoon readiness. Storm cleanup as needed. Basin and drainage inlets kept clear.

Monsoon season brings sudden wind, dust and heavy rain.

August

Storm response continues. Irrigation adjusted after heavy rain. Palm trimming wraps up.

Rain changes how much water each zone needs week to week.

September

bermuda. Fall pre-emergent. Storm damage repairs.

Scalping prepares turf for overseeding next month.

October

winter rye. Fall planting season opens. Run times reduced.

Warm soil and cooler air make October the best planting month in Phoenix.

November

Rye established and mowed. Planting continues. Holiday color for retail and entries.

The last stretch of easy growing weather before winter.

December

Frost cloth ready. Controllers to winter schedules. Year-end walkthrough and plan for next year.

A good time to agree on next year's enhancement priorities and budget.

Crew standards

Residents, tenants and customers see our crews on your property every week, so every crew follows the same standards for how they work.

Assigned routes
The same crew returns to your property, so they learn its systems and its problem spots.
Heat safety
Early starts in summer, scheduled water and shade breaks, and work planned around the hottest hours.
Appearance
Uniformed crews and marked trucks, so your residents and tenants know who's on site.
Courtesy
Walks and doorways kept clear while we work, and blowers pointed away from people and cars.
Languages
English and Spanish spoken by our leads.

OverseedSpreading ryegrass seed over dormant bermuda in fall, so the turf stays green through winter.
ScalpMowing bermuda very short in September, so new ryegrass seed can reach the soil.
Pre-emergentA weed treatment applied before weed seeds sprout. It keeps them from starting to grow.
BermudaThe warm-season grass on most Phoenix turf. It grows through summer and turns brown and dormant in winter.
Drip irrigationSmall tubes and emitters that deliver water slowly to each plant's roots. Used for shrubs, trees and beds.
Natural-form pruningPruning that keeps a plant's own shape by removing selected branches at their base.
Structural pruningPruning that builds a strong trunk and well-spaced branches, so the tree holds up in wind.
MonsoonArizona's summer storm season, June 15 to September 30, with high winds, dust storms and heavy rain.
TransitionThe late-spring period when winter ryegrass dies back in the heat and bermuda grows in again.
